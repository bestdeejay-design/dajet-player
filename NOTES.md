Dajet Player 0.3.0: owns the Mac's own controls. The project builds with SwiftPM, and `./build.sh` wraps the binary into an ordinary Mac app — `build/DajetPlayer.app`, `ru.dajet.player`, ad-hoc signed. Drop a folder of music — or a few files — on the window and it plays them one after another (mp3, m4a, flac, wav, aiff), its title, artist and album read from the file, its cover blurred behind the window. Next and Previous walk the pass (Previous restarting a track once its three seconds have gone, otherwise stepping back; on the first track it restarts), shuffle draws a fresh pass that never repeats a track twice, repeat cycles through off, all and one, and Space stops and starts. The keyboard's media keys are the app's — play, pause, play/pause, next and previous, each a hand on a button that was already there; the Now Playing card in Control Center and on the lock screen carries title, artist, duration and progress and goes away when nothing plays. A thin bar along the foot of the window shows where the track is and takes it where the hand lets go, once dragged; `←` and `→` walk the queue beside Space; volume and mute sit in the bar and are remembered between runs. Nothing in it can reach the network: no telemetry, no accounts, no cloud, no cover art fetched from somewhere. The two skins and the release are waves of their own. Since then, and not yet in a version: the first of the two skins, `dajet` — glass over the whole light window, matte near-black in the dark, a side menu of playlists that stays between runs, and the folder's tracks in that menu rather than in the middle of the window.
